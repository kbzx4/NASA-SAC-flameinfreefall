The gap

Every spacewalk happens in an atmosphere no microgravity fire test has come near.

NASA's open microgravity fire data stops at % oxygen. Crews have lived far past that: pure oxygen from Mercury to Apollo, three-quarters oxygen on Skylab, and pure oxygen inside every US spacesuit today. Apollo 1 burned in pure oxygen. Moon-base plans move back toward richer air, and solid materials, the things that actually burn in a cabin, were flame-tested in orbit only up to %.

What the data shows

Gravity decided whether silicone burned.

NASA's Saffire-II experiment burned the same materials in orbit and on the ground. NASA's challenge statement says the two results could bound what happens in between. This is where that bound is tight, and where it is wide open.

Gravity changed the outcome for of samples.

Thin silicone did not burn in microgravity and burned completely in normal gravity. Cotton-fiberglass fabric burned completely in both. The Moon and Mars lie somewhere along each bar, and no test says where.

MicrogravityNormal gravity (1g)

GSI, the Gravity Sensitivity Index, is the difference between the two burn lengths divided by the sample length. 0 means they agree; 1 means the sample burned fully in one and not at all in the other. The bound is a hypothesis: it assumes burning changes steadily with gravity. Source: PSI-99, Saffire-II.

Method

Built so you can check it.

Every number traces to NASA

Each record links to its page in NASA's Physical Science Informatics archive. Where we converted a unit, the original text sits beside the result. Where a NASA table is silent, the cell stays empty.

SI, checked by code

Code works out every finding and its ranking. A Gemini model then interprets the findings, reading only the facts code computed. Code looks up every number it wrote, a second model re-reads every sentence against the facts, and any sentence that fails either check is removed.

Honest about limits

Flamebound grades how much evidence exists near your conditions. It does not predict whether a material will burn, and it is not an operational safety system.
